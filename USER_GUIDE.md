# ROI Analysis Tool – User Guide (Full Version)

## 1. Purpose of the Tool

The ROI Analysis Tool supports data-driven investment decisions for new product initiatives. It standardizes financial evaluation across regions and enables consistent analysis of profitability, value creation, and risk using key metrics such as CM%, NPV, PI, and Payback period.

## 2. When to Use the Tool

### Scenario 1 – New Business Case Validation

Primary Users: Product Manager, Finance BP, BU Leadership  
Objective: Validate whether a project is financially viable before approval.  
Example: A new headphone project with MAP $99, expected volume 5,000,000 units, and FOB cost $20.  
Focus Areas: CM% vs target, NPV positive or not, PI above 1, and assumption realism.  
Decision Output: Approve, Conditional Approval, or Reject.

### Scenario 2 – Scenario & Sensitivity Analysis

Primary Users: Finance BP, Product Manager  
Objective: Evaluate how sensitive ROI results are to key assumptions such as price, volume, and cost.  
Example: Compare Base, Upside, and Downside scenarios to assess NPV stability.  
Focus Areas: NPV sensitivity, PI variation, Payback changes.  
Decision Output: Identify risk exposure and robustness of the business case.

### Scenario 3 – Cross-Region Profitability Comparison

Primary Users: Sales, Finance BP, BU Management  
Objective: Compare profitability across regions to identify weak markets.  
Example: US CM% at 50%, EMEA 20%, APAC 40% due to lower pricing.  
Focus Areas: CM% differences, pricing gaps, cost structure differences.  
Decision Output: Adjust regional pricing, volume, or strategy.

### Scenario 4 – Investment Prioritization

Primary Users: BU Head, Finance Director, VP/CFO  
Objective: Prioritize projects under limited investment resources.  
Example: Compare projects A, B, and C using NPV, PI, and Payback period.  
Focus Areas: Value creation through NPV, efficiency through PI, and risk through Payback.  
Decision Output: Portfolio selection and capital allocation.

### Scenario 5 – Business Case Challenge

Primary Users: Finance BP, GPLM, Sales  
Objective: Validate and challenge overly optimistic assumptions.  
Example: Sales assumes high volume and pricing, while downside scenario shows NPV turning negative.  
Focus Areas: Break-even point, margin sustainability, downside risk.  
Decision Output: Revise assumptions or escalate risks.

### Scenario 6 – Post-Launch Performance Tracking

Primary Users: Finance BP, Product Manager  
Objective: Compare actual performance vs original business case.  
Example: Actual volume lower and cost higher than planned reduces NPV.  
Focus Areas: Forecast deviation, updated ROI metrics.  
Decision Output: Adjust pricing, cost, or lifecycle strategy.

## 3. Key Decision Metrics

1. Contribution Margin % (CM%): Core profitability and margin quality indicator.  
2. Contribution Margin ($): Absolute profit contribution and scale impact.  
3. Net Present Value (NPV): Total value created considering time value of money. NPV above 0 indicates value creation.  
4. Profitability Index (PI): Capital efficiency metric. PI above 1 indicates a viable project.  
5. Payback Period: Time required to recover investment; shorter payback indicates lower risk.  
6. Price vs Cost Structure: Validates pricing realism and cost control.  
7. Volume Assumptions: Key driver of revenue and major uncertainty factor.  
8. Regional Variance: Identifies margin dilution across different markets.

## 4. What the Tool Helps Identify

- Margin risks due to low pricing or high cost
- Unrealistic assumptions in volume or price
- Structural weaknesses across regions
- Overall decision readiness, including approve, optimize, or reject

## 5. Best Practices

- Always validate key assumptions, especially price, volume, and cost
- Use multiple scenarios, including Base, Upside, and Downside
- Focus on margin quality, not just volume scale
- Use the tool as a decision discussion platform across teams

## 6. Summary

The ROI Analysis Tool transforms business case evaluation from assumption-based discussions into data-driven financial decisions, enabling better profitability management, risk control, and capital allocation.
